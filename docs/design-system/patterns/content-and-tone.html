Content and tone

The Debate App talks like a tidy assistant: brief, specific and calm, for members who read meetings and organisers who write them.

Overview

The Debate App talks to two people: members, who read the meeting list and the abstract, agenda and minutes, and organisers, who create and edit meetings. The voice is the same for both: a tidy assistant that says what is happening and what to do next. It is brief, specific and calm, and it never blames.

  • Plain and specific. Dates, times and counts are real: "Tomorrow · 7:00 pm–8:30 pm", "Fix 2 things to save".
  • Sentence case and real verbs on every button and title.
  • No blame, no drama. Errors say what did not happen and what is safe.

The full rules, formats and microcopy library are on Content; this page shows them applied to whole flows.

When to use

Use this pattern whenever you write words that ship: titles, labels, buttons, errors, empty states, toasts, dialogs and accessible names. Lift copy from the mocks before inventing new copy.

Structure

Every message is built from the same parts, in this order.

PartRuleExample
TitleWhat is true, in a few wordsWe couldn't load the meetings
DetailWhy, or what to do, in one or two sentencesCheck your connection and try again.
ReassuranceThat nothing is lost, when trueYour changes are still in the form.
ActionA verb that names the resultTry again · Back to meetings · Sign in
We couldn't sign you in
Check the password
A sign-in failure: the title says what failed, the detail says the fix, no word says which field was wrong. From pages/sign-in/invalid.

Responsive

  • Write titles that survive wrapping on a 360px phone: under 60 characters and no clever line breaks.
  • Toast and banner copy fits two lines at phone width; put the detail in the page, not the toast.
  • Never rely on a tooltip to explain a button; the label or aria-label must be enough.

Accessibility

  • Link and button text makes sense on its own (2.4.4): "Back to meetings", not "Click here".
  • Accessible names carry the same words as the visible label plus the object: "Edit Universal basic income".
  • Use plain language and short sentences; avoid idioms that do not translate.
  • Do not use emoji or symbols as the only carrier of meaning.
  • Set lang on the page; mark words in another language (such as a bilingual meeting title) when the language changes.
