Component · Containers

Card

A raised surface that holds one group of things: the sign-in form, a meeting's abstract, the list of upcoming meetings. Cards are plain surfaces; they never carry a status tint.

Overview

Use a card to group content that belongs together. A card is not a button: The Debate App has no clickable cards; actions inside a card are real buttons and links.

  • Card (.card): 24px padding. The sign-in form, a skeleton placeholder, a settings group.
  • Prose card (.card.prose): a card holding rendered rich text, such as a meeting's abstract, agenda or minutes inside a tab panel. See Prose.
  • Flush (.card--flush): no padding, clips its content. Wraps a list of meeting rows edge to edge (.list.card.card--flush).
  • Title (.card__title) goes inside when the card needs a heading of its own.

The base stylesheet also defines .card--success, .card--warning, cover tints and a stat block. No Debate App mock uses them, so they are not part of this system.

Anatomy

  1. Surface--color-bg-surface, hairline --color-border-default, --radius-lg, --shadow-1, --space-6 padding.
  2. Title.card__title in --text-h4 on a real heading (h2 or h3). Optional.
  3. ContentUsually a .stack, a .prose body, or a .list of rows.

Sizes

One padding (--space-6), or none with .card--flush. Width comes from the layout it sits in; the sign-in card is held to 26rem by .auth-card.

States

Cards are not interactive, so they have no hover, focus or pressed state. What changes is their content: loading shows skeletons in the same card, and empty shows an empty state inside it.

Responsive behaviour

  • Cards fill their column. On phones every card is full width and stacks; the page content column is capped by the layout primitives.
  • Padding stays 24px at every width so content lines up with the page header.
  • Content inside uses .stack and .cluster and wraps. Code blocks in a prose card scroll inside the card.

Theming

The card reads one component token, --card-bg (default --color-bg-surface). In the dark theme the surface darkens and --shadow-1 deepens; the hairline border keeps the edge visible.

/* A card on a sunken area can re-point the token without new CSS */
.card { --card-bg: var(--color-bg-surface); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A <section aria-labelledby> when the card is a named region of the page (it becomes a region landmark), an <article> for a self-contained item, a <form> or <fieldset> when it is a form. A card inside a tab panel needs no landmark: the panel is already labelled by its tab. No ARIA role on the class itself.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabThe card is not focusable; focus moves through the links and buttons inside it.

Focus

.card--flush clips its content (overflow: hidden); controls inside keep the 2px ring visible because meeting rows are padded by --space-4.

Labelling

The .card__title is a real heading at the right level and names the section through aria-labelledby.

Contrast

Text on card
Muted text on card

The card edge (hairline border on canvas) is decorative and not required to meet 3:1.

Motion

None, except the sign-in card, which scales in once (.auth-card .card) and stops for prefers-reduced-motion.

Touch

The card itself is not a target. Targets inside follow their own rules (44px buttons, 24px minimum).

Content

  • Titles are short and specific, in sentence case: "Sign in", "Upcoming", "Past".
  • A prose card has no title of its own; the tab label above it names it ("Abstract", "Agenda", "Minutes").
  • Meeting names are used verbatim: "Should cities ban cars from downtown?".
