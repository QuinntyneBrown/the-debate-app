Overview

The Debate App talks to two people. Members read the meeting list and the abstract, agenda and minutes of a meeting, so their copy is short, welcoming and specific. Organisers create and edit meetings, often between other things, so their copy is brief and gets them to the next action. Both get the same plain-English rules: sentence case, real verbs, specific dates and times, no blame.

Lift copy from the mocks before writing new copy; the microcopy library below is the starting point. The pattern page Content and tone shows these rules applied to whole flows.

Sentence case and punctuation

  • Sentence case everywhere: titles, buttons, labels, menu items, toasts. Only the first word and names are capitalised: "New meeting", "Delete meeting", "Should cities ban cars from downtown?".
  • Motions keep the wording the organiser typed: "This house would introduce a universal basic income."
  • Curly quotes and apostrophes in product copy: ’ “ ”. Meeting names in running text are plain; in a dialog title they go in quotes: Delete “Universal basic income”?
  • No full stop on titles, labels, buttons or single-phrase toasts; full stops on sentences in help, error and toast text.
  • Middle dot separates short facts on one line: "Tomorrow · 7:00 pm–8:30 pm".
  • Contractions are fine and friendlier: "didn’t", "you’re", "can’t".
  • "E-mail" with a hyphen; "sign in" (verb) and "sign-in" (noun).

  • 12-hour clock with a space and lower-case "am" or "pm": "4:12 pm".
  • Short day and month names, day before month, no comma between them: "Fri 17 Oct". No year in the current year.
  • Digits for all numbers in lists and status text, including one to nine, because people scan them: "2 upcoming", "8 min".
  • Plurals follow the number: "1 meeting", "2 meetings".

Error messages

Formula: what happened (or what to do), why if it helps, and that nothing is lost whenever that is true. Never blame the person, never say "invalid", "error" or "failed" on their own, and always offer the way forward.

WhereFormulaExample
Field errorWhat to do, then why or how (one or two short sentences)"Enter a name." · "End must be after start."
Error summary"Fix N things to save" / "One thing to fix", then a linked list"Fix 2 things to save"
Failed action (dialog, toast)What did not happen + why + that nothing is lost"The meeting wasn’t deleted" · "The Debate App couldn’t reach the server. Nothing has changed; try again in a moment."
Failed load"We couldn’t load [thing]" + the fix + Try again"We couldn’t load the meetings" · "Check your connection and try again."
Wrong username or passwordOne generic sentence, never saying which was wrong"Username or password is wrong."
Expired sessionSay what happened, offer sign-in"Your session may have ended." · "Sign in to continue"
Server errorNot your fault + try again + who to tell"Something went wrong on our side" · "Try again in a moment. If it keeps happening, tell Jordan."

Empty states

Formula: a title that says what is here (not "No data"), one or two sentences on why it is empty and what will fill it, and one action that fills it.

Usage

  • Use the names from the club: "member", "organiser", and the person’s own name where known. Never "the user" or "the record".
  • Talk about meetings, abstracts, agendas and minutes, not "items" or "entries".
  • Say what happened and what to do next; do not celebrate or scold.
  • Keep a toast to a title plus one sentence; keep a field error to two short sentences.
  • Write the accessible name as carefully as the visible text; read it aloud.

Accessibility

  • Plain language with short sentences helps everyone, including people reading in a second language.
  • Link and button text stands alone (2.4.4): "Back to meetings", not "Click here".
  • Do not rely on emoji; the sentence must make sense without them. The Debate App uses none in product copy.
  • Counts in accessible names say what they count: "2 upcoming meetings", not "2".
